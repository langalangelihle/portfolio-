Question 1 — Overview of BIS (LU1)

Q.1.1 (4 marks)
Explain the difference between data and information, and provide one example of each in a business context.
Data = raw, unprocessed facts that do not support decision-making on their own. Example: the number 450 in a spreadsheet cell. Information = data that has been processed, organised, and given context to support decision-making. Example: "Total sales for March: R450 000" Key distinction: data is INPUT; information is the OUTPUT after processing.
Q.1.2 (5 marks)
Identify at least five basic resources required to support Business Information Systems (BIS).
1. People resources - managers, IT staff, and end users 2. Hardware resources - computers, servers, printers, smartphones, storage media 3. Software resources - programs, instruction manuals, company policies 4. Communications resources - networks, cables, Wi-Fi hardware and software 5. Data resources - computer databases, paper files, sensor data, customer records
Q.1.3 (6 marks)
Explain how an organisation such as Amazon could use any of Michael Porter's three strategies to gain a competitive advantage over its competitors.
1. Cost Leadership - Amazon automates warehouses, offers digital delivery (Kindle, Prime Video), and emails statements instead of printing. Lower operational costs = lower prices = competitive edge. 2. Differentiation - Amazon Prime offers unique features (same-day delivery, exclusive streaming, early deals) that competitors cannot easily replicate, building strong customer loyalty. 3. Focus - Amazon Web Services (AWS) targets businesses needing cloud computing, not general consumers. Dominating this niche gives Amazon a massive advantage in enterprise cloud services. (Any one or two strategies with relevant examples will score marks.)
Q.1.4 (6 marks)
Briefly discuss at least three characteristics of computer system components that may impact a computer system's performance.
1. Number and size of processors - more cores (dual, quad, octa) increase parallel processing. A 64-bit processor handles data more efficiently than a 32-bit one. 2. Bus component - the bus transfers data between CPU and hardware. A wider bus carries more data per cycle, increasing speed. 3. Clock speed - measured in MHz or GHz. Higher clock speed = more instructions processed per second. 4. RAM - more RAM allows more data in fast-access storage, reducing delays from reading secondary storage. 5. Storage type - SSDs are faster than HDDs, impacting OS and application load times.
Q.1.5 (5 marks)
Describe Porter's Five Forces model. Briefly explain each force.
Porter's Five Forces is used to analyse an organisation's competitive position and determine the best IS strategy. 1. Buyer Power - HIGH when customers have many choices. Reduce through differentiation and loyalty programmes. 2. Supplier Power - HIGH when customers have few alternative suppliers. Internet access has increased buyer options, reducing supplier power. 3. Threat of Substitute Products - when competitors offer similar products, organisations add value and features to differentiate. 4. Threat of New Entrants - organisations use focus strategy to make it difficult for others to replicate their products. 5. Rivalry Among Competitors - increases as more competitors enter. Managed by offering superior value-added services.
Q.1.6 (4 marks)
Identify the three levels of management and state which type of information system is used at each level.
1. Strategic level (Top/Senior management) IS used: EIS (Executive Information System) and DSS (Decision Support System) Purpose: long-term, unstructured strategic decisions 2. Tactical level (Middle management) IS used: MIS / IRS (Information Reporting System) and DSS Purpose: semi-structured planning and control decisions 3. Operational level (Supervisors / front-line workers) IS used: TPS (Transaction Processing System) and OAS (Office Automation System) Purpose: structured, day-to-day routine transactions

Question 2 — Enterprise & Functional IS (LU2)

Q.2.1 (4 marks)
Briefly describe how an enterprise system functions.
Enterprise systems support business processes across ALL functional boundaries. They: - Use Internet technology to integrate information internally and with external stakeholders - Feature a centralised database structure for seamless data sharing across all departments - Have defined business process blueprints (e.g. procurement, manufacturing, sales) - Provide a cross-functional process view of the entire organisation Examples: SAP ERP, Microsoft Dynamics
Q.2.2 (5 marks)
Identify at least five important features of Executive Information Systems (EIS).
1. Provides summary information for monitoring business performance 2. Primarily used for strategic decision-making (also supports tactical decisions) 3. Offers drill-down capability to access detailed underlying information 4. Includes analysis tools for data interpretation 5. Integrates with other systems for problem-solving and daily operations 6. Consolidates data from various internal AND external sources 7. Tailored user-friendly design for non-computer-savvy senior managers
Q.2.3 (5 marks)
Discuss the differences between a Transaction Processing System (TPS) and a Management Information System (MIS/IRS).
TPS (Transaction Processing System): - Handles day-to-day operational transactions (sales, purchases, payroll) - Used at the operational level of management - Processes high volumes of structured, routine data - Example: POS system at a supermarket MIS / IRS (Management/Information Reporting System): - Generates reports from TPS data to support middle management decisions - Used for tactical and planning decisions - Produces periodic reports (monthly financials) and exception reports (threshold breaches) - Example: monthly sales summary report Relationship: MIS relies on data captured by TPS. One feeds the other.
Q.2.4 (6 marks)
Explain how a CRM system benefits an organisation. Include three CRM applications.
CRM systems are centred around a customer database and help manage marketing, sales, and service. Three CRM applications: 1. Customer data collection - gathers personal details (age, gender, contact) and transaction history (location, date, quantity, price) to personalise service. 2. Customer data analysis - categorises and targets customers based on criteria, enhancing marketing campaign effectiveness. 3. Salesforce automation - manages the full sales cycle from lead generation through to closing and after-sales service. e-CRM: when employees and customers access the CRM database through a website. Benefit: improved customer loyalty, personalised service, more effective marketing.

Question 3 — The Machine Behind Computing (LU3)

Q.3.1 (5 marks)
Identify and describe the main components of a computer system.
1. Input devices - send data to the computer (keyboard, mouse, scanner, barcode reader, microphone) 2. Main memory (RAM) - volatile, temporarily holds data and instructions for CPU 3. CPU - the brain; contains ALU (arithmetic/logic) and Control Unit (manages data flow) 4. Secondary storage - permanent data storage (HDD, SSD, USB flash drive, optical disc) 5. Output devices - present processed data to user (monitor, printer, speakers, projector) Additional: motherboard, disk drives, ports, buses, adapter cards, CPU case
Q.3.2 (6 marks)
Distinguish between the six classes of computers.
1. Supercomputers - most powerful; weather forecasting, nuclear research, protein folding. Speed in petaFLOPS. Example: IBM Blue Gene/L 2. Mainframes - large organisational computers; process millions of instructions/second; banks and governments; expensive; require HVAC data centres 3. Minicomputers/Servers - manage network resources; types: application, database, file, mail, print, web, RAS servers 4. Personal Computers (PCs) - home and small business use; performance depends on processor speed and RAM 5. Notebooks/Laptops - portable, battery-powered; same capabilities as desktop PCs 6. Subnotebooks - smaller than laptops; no optical drives
Q.3.3 (5 marks)
Describe the five generations of programming languages, providing an example of each.
1GL - Machine language: binary code (0s and 1s); directly executed by CPU; no translation needed. Example: 01001000 2GL - Assembly language: uses symbolic names (mnemonics); requires assembler to translate. Example: MOV AX, 1 3GL - High-level language: human-readable; requires compiler/interpreter. Examples: C, Java, Python, C++ 4GL - Very high-level: closer to natural language; used for database queries. Examples: SQL, MATLAB, SAS 5GL - Natural/AI language: uses constraints to solve problems; used in AI. Examples: Prolog, LISP
Q.3.4 (5 marks)
Identify the five characteristics that must be present for a solution to qualify as cloud computing, and name two cloud storage providers.
5 Cloud Characteristics: 1. On-demand self-service - users access resources without intermediary assistance 2. Broad network access - accessible from any device/location 3. Resource pooling - unused services shared with other users 4. Rapid elasticity - environment expands quickly in response to demand 5. Measured service - users can measure and track their service usage Cloud storage providers: Amazon AWS (Glacier), Google Cloud Storage, Microsoft Azure

Question 4 — Database Systems and Analytics (LU4)

Q.4.1 (5 marks)
Describe the five components of a Database Management System (DBMS).
1. Database Engine - the heart of the DBMS; supports storage, manipulation, and retrieval of data 2. Data Definition - maintains the data dictionary; defines structure of files, field properties, primary keys, and relationships using DDL (Data Definition Language) 3. Data Manipulation - adds, deletes, modifies, retrieves records using SQL (Structured Query Language) or QBE (Query By Example) 4. Application Generation - creates user-friendly screens and report menus for data entry; used by IT professionals and DBAs 5. Data Administration - handles backup/recovery, security, change management, and CRUD privileges; managed by the Database Administrator (DBA)
Q.4.2 (5 marks)
Describe the data hierarchy from a bit to a database, providing an example at each level.
Bit -> Character/Byte -> Field -> Record -> File -> Database Example (customer database): - Bit: smallest unit - 0 or 1 - Character: single letter e.g. "C" in "Cust01" - Field: complete value "Cust01" (customer number) - Record: entire row - Cust01, John, Doe - File: all customer records in the Customer table - Database: Customer table + Orders table linked together
Q.4.3 (3 marks)
What is the difference between a data warehouse and a data mart?
Data warehouse: large, organisation-wide repository that stores historical data from ALL departments and sources. Used for strategic business intelligence. Data mart: a SUBSET of a data warehouse. Smaller and focused on a specific department or function only (e.g. only sales data or only HR data). In short: data mart is contained within and is a part of the data warehouse.
Q.4.4 (4 marks)
Describe the four characteristics of Big Data (the 4 Vs) and name two Big Data tools.
1. Volume - enormous amounts of data (terabytes/petabytes daily from transactions, social media, IoT) 2. Variety - structured (tables), semi-structured (XML, JSON), unstructured (videos, social posts, images) 3. Velocity - speed at which data must be collected and processed, often in real time 4. Veracity - messiness/quality challenges (Twitter abbreviations, typos, speech patterns) Big Data tools: Apache Hadoop, NoSQL, Cassandra, SAP Big Data Analytics, Tableau, SAS
Q.4.5 (3 marks)
Briefly describe the three types of business analytics.
1. Descriptive analytics - analyses historical data to answer "What happened?" Example: monthly sales report showing revenue dropped 10% in March. 2. Predictive analytics - uses statistical models to answer "What might happen?" Example: forecasting that sales will drop 15% next quarter based on trends. 3. Prescriptive analytics - recommends actions to answer "What should we do?" Example: recommending a 20% discount promotion to prevent the forecasted sales drop.

Question 5 — Networks, Telecommunications & Internet (LU5)

Q.5.1 (7 marks)
Name and describe the function of each of the seven layers of the OSI model.
7. Application Layer - supports user tasks: email, file transfers, database access, web browsing 6. Presentation Layer - formats message packets for compatibility between different systems 5. Session Layer - establishes, manages, and terminates communication sessions between computers 4. Transport Layer - ensures messages delivered without error, in sequence, with no loss or duplication 3. Network Layer - responsible for ROUTING messages through the network 2. Data Link Layer - oversees establishment and control of the communication link 1. Physical Layer - transmits raw binary data (bits) over the physical medium Memory tip (top to bottom): "All People Seem To Need Data Processing"
Q.5.2 (5 marks)
Describe the four main network topologies. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of each.
1. Star - all devices connect to a central hub/switch Advantage: one cable failure only affects that device Disadvantage: central hub failure = entire network down 2. Ring - devices connected in a circular loop; data travels one direction Advantage: reduces data collisions Disadvantage: one device failure can disrupt the entire ring 3. Bus - all devices share one common backbone cable Advantage: cheap and simple to set up for small networks Disadvantage: main cable failure = entire network down 4. Mesh - every device connects to every other device Advantage: highest fault tolerance; multiple data paths Disadvantage: most expensive and complex to install and manage
Q.5.3 (5 marks)
Describe five transmission media used in telecommunications and briefly state one characteristic of each.
1. Twisted pair cable - two insulated copper wires twisted together; cheap, widely used in LANs (Cat5e, Cat6); susceptible to interference 2. Coaxial cable - central copper conductor surrounded by insulation and shielding; better noise resistance than twisted pair; used in cable TV and older networks 3. Fibre optic cable - transmits data as pulses of light through glass/plastic fibres; fastest transmission; immune to electromagnetic interference; expensive 4. Microwave transmission - wireless; uses high-frequency radio waves; requires line of sight between towers; used for long-distance point-to-point links 5. Satellite transmission - signals sent to orbiting satellite and back; covers vast geographic areas; used for remote areas; higher latency than terrestrial media
Q.5.4 (4 marks)
Identify four benefits and two disadvantages of using computer networks in an organisation.
Benefits: 1. Lower transaction costs - less human input required due to automation 2. Improved sharing of information and hardware resources (e.g. shared printer, files) 3. Supports virtual organisations and remote working 4. Enables just-in-time delivery by connecting systems to real-time inventory data 5. Facilitates communication with customers, suppliers, and partners globally Disadvantages: 1. Over-reliance - if the network fails, mission-critical operations are disrupted 2. High initial setup costs for infrastructure, hardware, and ongoing administration
Q.5.5 (4 marks)
Explain what a VPN is and describe two benefits of using a VPN in an organisation.
VPN (Virtual Private Network): extends a private network securely across a public network (the Internet) using encryption and tunnelling protocols. It allows users to access organisational resources remotely as if they were physically on the internal network. Two benefits: 1. Secure remote access - employees working from home or travelling can access internal systems securely using encrypted tunnels, protecting sensitive data from interception. 2. Cost savings - instead of expensive leased private lines between offices, organisations can use the public Internet with VPN encryption, significantly reducing WAN costs.
